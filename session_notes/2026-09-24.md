**Items 1 and 6 — Path 3 two-sample correction and truncation-bias bound:
derived and implemented.** Dispatched Oracle for full derivations of (1) the
two-sample correction when the target covariate sample is genuinely external
(size `n*`, independent of the source) and (6) the truncation-bias bound when
capping a heavy-tailed transport weight `w` at `C`. Both derivations came back
as rigorous propositions with proofs (available in the Oracle session; not
yet transcribed into `main.tex` — see Next). Headline results:

- **Two-sample variance addendum.** `V = V_src + ρ·Var_Q(τ)`, `ρ = lim n/n*`,
  with constant *exactly* 1 (an equality, confirmed by the audit, not an order
  bound) — this validates the informal guess that motivated dispatching the
  derivation. Implemented as `integrate_cate(..., n_target = n_star)`: adds
  `ρ̂·Var̂_Q(τ)/n` to the variance (point estimate untouched), where `ρ̂ = n/
  n_target` and `Var̂_Q(τ)` is the weighted second moment of `τ` under the
  (possibly truncated) final weights. Ignored, with a warning, for Form A
  (`target`), where the "target" is a fixed subsample of the source and there
  is no second sample.
- **Truncation bias bound.** `|B̃(C)| ≤ {Q(w>C)}^{1/2}/(1-δ_C) · {Var_Q(τ)}^
  {1/2}` (Cauchy–Schwarz form), where `δ_C = 1 - E_src[w∧C]` is the target
  weight-mass discarded by the cap — **exactly zero when `τ` is constant on
  the truncated region**, regardless of `C`. Implemented as
  `integrate_cate(..., trunc = C)` (fixed numeric cap, `≥ 1`) or
  `trunc = "auto"` (grid search for the smallest cap on `trunc_grid` whose
  bound is at most `trunc_gamma` × SE, default 0.25 — an
  Armstrong–Kolesár-style bias-aware budget). Guards: `trunc < 1` errors
  (since `E_src[w] = 1`, capping below 1 always discards mass for nothing);
  `δ_C > 0.5` errors (the capped estimand no longer refers to the intended
  target). Diagnostics (`delta_trunc`, `q_exceed`, `var_q_tau`, `bias_bound`,
  `se_bias_aware`) returned on the `cate_integration` object and shown by
  `print()`.

  **Implementation subtlety worth flagging for future readers of this code:**
  `Var_Q(τ)` in both features must be centered at the *plug-in weighted mean
  of τ itself* (`Σwτ/Σw`), not at the doubly-robust one-step point estimate
  `ψ̂` — they coincide only in the population, not in a finite noisy sample
  (the DR correction term has population mean zero but not sample mean zero).
  Centering at `ψ̂` breaks the "exactly zero under homogeneity" property in
  finite samples; a first implementation attempt did exactly this and was
  caught by a test asserting the zero-bias property with `τ` set literally
  constant. Fixed by making `.weighted_var_q(w, tau)` self-contained (no
  `psi` argument) — see `R/integrate_cate.R`.

  New tests appended to `tests/testthat/test-integrate-cate.R` (14 new
  `test_that` blocks): no-op at `trunc = Inf`, SE reduction under a lognormal
  heavy tail, exact-zero bias under constant `τ`, `trunc = "auto"` respecting
  its budget, the `trunc ≥ 1` and `δ_C ≤ 0.5` guards, the two-sample addendum's
  magnitude and its Form A no-op warning, `n_target > 0` validation,
  exact-zero addendum under constant `τ`, and composition (truncation feeding
  the correct final weights into the two-sample addendum). Full package test
  suite re-run clean throughout (0 failures; only the pre-existing
  mock-object warnings/skips).

**Item 2 — DiD score for (g,t)-indexed staggered adoption: derived, NOT yet
incorporated.** Oracle's derivation (not-yet-treated comparison group,
per-cell EIF, aggregation across cells) came back complete but flags a real
subtlety a rushed implementation would get wrong: the cellwise-efficient
aggregation is **not** semiparametrically efficient for `θ_{p+1}` itself
(only "GMM-efficient among linear combinations of the chosen cell scores"),
and small cohorts can dominate the two-sample variance term when the
not-yet-treated comparison set overlaps a target sample. Implementing this
correctly requires a genuinely new multi-cell score-matrix architecture (the
package currently has no notion of "a family of (g,t) cells sharing base
periods/comparison units with correlated scores"). Given the size of that
change and the risk of introducing exactly the kind of subtle bug this
session spent most of its time finding and fixing elsewhere, **deferred**:
theory is in hand (Oracle session, retrievable), but no `main.tex` prose and
no package code were written for this item today.
